    Willkommen. Bienvenue. Welcome.

    Mesdames et messieurs, ladies and gentlemen, Damen und Herren —

    Come in. Sit down. Leave your expensive staples at the door.

    Here, in this little Commander format, we do not ask what is the best deck you can build. We ask something far more interesting: what can you do with 65 euros, a color combination you did not choose, and a commander nobody saw coming?

    You see, in the outside world, players spend hundreds on their decks. They tutor for the same cards. They play the same commanders. How... boring.

    But here? Here we have rules. Here we have drama. One player whispers a wedge into your ear like a secret. And you — oh, you poor, wonderful, creative soul — must build something magnificent from whatever scraps that wedge allows. No Demonic Tutors. No famous generals riding to your rescue. Just your wits, your collection, and sixty-five euros.

    The money is not the point, of course. The point is the constraint. The point is the reveal. The point is sitting down across from three other people who were all given a cage and asked to make art inside it.

    So. Are you ready to play?

    Then let us begin.

    How a Challenge Works

    1. Challengers and challenged players are assigned randomly (Secret Santa draw).
    2. The challenger picks any of the 10 three-color wedges and presents it to their assigned challenged player. Deadline: 10 days after the Secret Santa is drawn.
    3. The challenged chooses any color combination within that wedge — mono-color, two-color, or the full three — and builds a deck respecting all rules below.
    4. On game night, everyone reveals their commander and brew, and all four players sit down and play.

    Deck submission deadline: August 1st, 2026. You have two options: - Submit a Moxfield decklist link before the deadline, plus a screenshot of the Moxfield price total at the time of brewing to prove the budget constraint was met. - Alternatively, bring a screenshot of your Moxfield decklist to the first match session showing the price at time of brewing. You then reveal your commander and deck on the spot.

    The Rules

    Budget: 65 €

    The total cost of the deck may not exceed 65 €. Price is determined by the Moxfield decklist total using the cheapest available printing of each card. Basic lands are free and do not count toward the budget.

    Pricing baseline: Cardmarket. Make sure Moxfield uses Cardmarket as its primary price source. To set it:

    Profile → Account Settings → Pricing & Affiliates → drag Cardmarket to the top of the list.

    Color Wedge

    The challenger assigns a three-color wedge. The challenged may build with any color combination contained within that wedge — but cannot play a colorless commander.

    For example, if the assigned wedge is Jund (Black/Red/Green), valid commander color identities are:

    • Mono-Black, Mono-Red, or Mono-Green
    • Rakdos (BR), Golgari (BG), or Gruul (RG)
    • Full Jund (BRG)

    The commander defines the deck's color identity as usual — cards in the 99 must fall within the commander's colors.

    This ban applies only to the commander slot — those cards can still appear in the 99 if they are legal in the deck's color identity and fit the budget.

    Target Bracket: 2

    Decks should be built to Bracket 2 of the official Commander bracket system. This means:

    • No game changers (as defined by the official bracket rules)
    • No mass land destruction
    • No extra turn spells as a primary strategy
    • Decks aim for a fun, interactive game rather than a fast or oppressive win

    Bracket 2: Core

    Experience: The easiest reference point is that the average current preconstructed deck is at a Core (Bracket 2) level.

    While Bracket 2 decks may not have every perfect card, they have the potential for big, splashy turns, strong engines, and are built in a way that works toward winning the game. While the game is unlikely to end out of nowhere and generally goes nine or more turns, you can expect big swings. The deck usually has some cards that aren't perfect from a gameplay perspective but are there for flavor reasons, or just because they bring a smile to your face.

    Deck Building: No cards from the Game Changers list. No intentional two-card infinite combos or mass land denial. Extra-turn cards should only appear in low quantities and are not intended to be chained in succession or looped. Tutors should be sparse.

    — Introducing Commander Brackets (Beta)

    Tutor Ban

    Free tutoring undermines the creative constraint of the format. The following cards are banned in all decks:

    • Demonic Tutor
    • Vampiric Tutor
    • Imperial Seal
    • Grim Tutor
    • Diabolic Tutor
    • Dark Petition
    • Mastermind's Acquisition
    • Beseech the Mirror
    • Worldly Tutor
    • Sylvan Tutor
    • Mystical Tutor
    • Personal Tutor
    • Solve the Equation
    • Enlightened Tutor
    • Entomb
    • Gamble
    • Scheming Symmetry
    • Wishclaw Talisman
    • Natural Order
    • Diabolic Intent
    • Unmarked Grave
    • Intuition
    • Survival of the Fittest
    • Eladamri's Call
    • Cruel Tutor

    Tutors with a genuine constraint beyond card type are legal. A constraint means something that meaningfully limits the search — a CMC cap, a power/toughness restriction, a subtype, or a destination other than hand. Examples of legal tutors:

    Card Why it's legal
    Trinket Mage Artifact with CMC ≤ 1 only
    Recruiter of the Guard Creature with power ≤ 1 only
    Fierce Empath Creature with CMC ≥ 6 only
    Buried Alive Creatures only; goes to graveyard
    Fauna Shaman Creature; requires discarding a creature
    Chord of Calling Creature with CMC ≤ X
    Finale of Devastation Creature with CMC ≤ X

    The Spirit of the Format

    The rules exist to create interesting deckbuilding, not to be exploited. When in doubt, ask: does this card let me ignore the creative constraint? If yes, it probably shouldn't be in the deck.

    The best games in this format come from unexpected commanders doing surprising things on a shoestring budget — not from someone optimizing around the edges of the rules.

    Have fun, brew something weird, and show what 65 € can do.
